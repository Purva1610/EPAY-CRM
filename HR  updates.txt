Absolutely. The right way to design this is not to assume employees are dishonest, but to build an HR system where claims are evidence-based, cross-verified, auditable, and difficult to manipulate.

For your ERP + CRM, I would build a “HR Verification & Integrity Layer” on top of attendance, leave, tasks, expenses, performance and employee requests.

1. First principle: don't let HR accept "silly" answers

Think from the employee's perspective:

“What excuses could I give HR that sound reasonable but are difficult to verify?”

Your CRM should be designed around those failure points.

Common employee claims your system should flag
Employee says	Possible issue	CRM verification
“I was sick.”	No medical evidence	Medical document required based on policy
“Doctor advised rest.”	Fake/old document	Document metadata + date + consistency checks
“Family emergency.”	Vague explanation	Required supporting evidence where appropriate
“My internet was down.”	Avoiding work	Network/attendance/task timeline comparison
“Laptop stopped working.”	Excuse for inactivity	IT ticket + device status + resolution time
“I completed the task.”	Task not actually complete	Deliverable + version history + reviewer confirmation
“Client didn't respond.”	False progress claim	CRM communication history

“I sent the WhatsApp.”	False communication claim	Approved business-channel activity log

If the employee still doesn't improve

Don't simply let HR click Terminate.

Move the employee to:

Final Performance Review

The system assembles the entire case automatically:

Performance evidence + warnings + HR calls + 1-to-1 meetings + employee responses + PIP + improvement results + manager feedback

Then the authorized decision-maker chooses the appropriate outcome, including separation where permitted.

This gives you a very strong principle for the HR CRM:

Every employee should get a documented opportunity to understand the problem, explain the reason, receive reasonable support, and demonstrate improvement before serious employment action is considered—unless a separate policy or serious misconduct requires a different process.
